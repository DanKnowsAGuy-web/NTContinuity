The Problem

A Credential Wearing
the Clothes of a Method

You have read the same passage with someone from the same tradition, using the same tools, and watched them arrive at a completely different conclusion. You cited scholars. They cited scholars. Nobody moved.

This is not a personality problem or a lack of study. The grammatical-historical method, which everyone claims to use, has no agreed standard for what counts as evidence, no way to track where scholars actually agree, and no mechanism to test whether a conclusion could be wrong.

In practice, an interpreter finds the conclusion they find most convincing, locates scholars whose framing supports it, and cites those scholars as independent confirmation. The loop closes on itself.

Nothing inside the method breaks it open.

Textual criticism asks what the text originally said. That question is treated as a science: thousands of manuscript variants, carefully weighed, documented, contested in peer-reviewed literature, tracked over generations.

Hermeneutics asks what the text means. That question is treated as an opinion.

Careful scholars have produced profound work within this framework. The framework itself has been coasting on reputation rather than mechanism for a long time. The NT Continuity Project is building the mechanism.

What We Are Building

Three Structures.
One Infrastructure.

I

Conferences

Scholars from genuinely different traditions engage the same texts with the same transparency requirements applied to all of them. Live cross-examination. Visible presuppositions. Arguments that have to survive contact with people who do not share your tradition's commitments. Regional gatherings bring the conversation to pastors, educators, and serious lay readers.

II

A Quarterly Journal

Each issue is a structured conversation: multiple contributors from different traditions, one question, the same transparency requirements applied to all of them. Every argument must name its tradition, state its presuppositions, show its chain of continuity, and state what evidence would require revision.

III

A Living Database

Every peer-reviewed data point enters a structured, searchable record. The software built on it surfaces the full evidentiary base for any argument, stress-tests its load-bearing claims, maps the counterarguments, and returns a probability profile showing exactly where a reading is strong, contested, or dependent on unverified assumptions.

The Database

What the Software
Actually Does

No commentary can do what this database does. It shows you the full landscape of evidence for any interpretive argument, including data you have not considered. It returns not a verdict but a probability profile: where your reading is strong, where it is contested, and what it depends on that has not been independently verified.

The output for many questions will be contested or underdetermined. That is it working correctly. Honesty about uncertainty is more useful than false confidence.

The Scope of Evidence

Meaning Does Not Begin
at the Moment of Writing

The project traces continuity across the full arc of the tradition's reception. A reading that cannot survive contact with the breadth of that record is telling you something. The dominant methodological conversations have been conducted largely in German, English, and French, inside Western academic institutions.

The texts were not written in the West. The earliest interpreters were not Western. Syriac Christianity. The Coptic and Ethiopian traditions. The Armenian church. Asian and African theological development, ancient and contemporary. These are not supplements to the main conversation. They are part of the evidentiary base.

Period I
Ancient Near Eastern World

The conceptual vocabulary of the Hebrew scriptures does not emerge from nowhere. Creation, covenant, kingship, temple, sacrifice: these categories arrive in the biblical text already carrying semantic weight.

Period II
Second Temple Judaism

The world in which the New Testament was written. The debates, the expectations, the contested categories, the diversity of Jewish thought between the Testaments: all of this is the immediate context.

Period III
The New Testament Era

The texts themselves, read with the full weight of what preceded them and with attention to how they were received by those who first read them.

Periods IV–VI
Early Church Through the Modern Era

Pre-Constantinian, pre-Schism, premodern, and modern, including the global traditions that developed outside the European stream.

What Changes

Disagreement Becomes
Legible

Two scholars who disagree but are looking at the same probability profile are having a different conversation than two scholars citing competing authorities at each other.

Before

"The scholars I have consulted are clear on this. Your scholars are wrong."

Presuppositions hidden. Evidence selectively presented. No mechanism to test the claim. No path forward.

After

"I hold this tentatively. The earliest reception is ambiguous and my tradition has a strong interest in one reading over the other."

Presuppositions named. Confidence earned rather than claimed. Weaknesses visible. The conversation can actually go somewhere.

The goal is not to end disagreement. It is to make disagreement worth having. To raise the floor of accountability so that when two people read the same text and reach different conclusions, they can identify precisely where they diverge and why.
